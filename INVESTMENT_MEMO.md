# When contracted revenue deserves a closer look

**Illustrative memo · Fictional business**

## Decision

**Keep the business on the research list.** Before advancing, check whether renewals require meaningful concessions or mostly follow from customers continuing to use the product.

## Thesis

A fictional software provider helps industrial operators prepare required inspection records. Management argues that customers renew because changing providers would mean rebuilding the inspection history and retraining staff. If that friction survives procurement review, the installed base could support durable cash generation.

## The strongest opposing case

Customer teams could depend on the workflow while procurement still has bargaining power. An incumbent might keep the account by discounting renewals, including support at no extra charge, or agreeing to costly custom work. In that case, retention would conceal weak economics.

| Question | What I would examine | What would change the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the product create switching costs? | An end-to-end migration, including data export, customer signoff, and retraining | A practical migration path would weaken the thesis even if users like the product |
| Can the company renew without buying the renewal? | Renewal terms, scope changes, and concessions compared with the original contracts | Recurring concessions would shift diligence toward competitive pressure |
| Does servicing the customer get easier after onboarding? | The split between reusable implementation work and custom support | Persistent manual support would make revenue quality less attractive |

## Valuation implication

The case for a premium depends on recurring revenue turning into recurring cash flow. I would examine renewal concessions and servicing effort before making that assumption. The downside case is a company that retains customers but absorbs the cost of doing so through narrower margins.

## Reopen the decision when

There is direct evidence from contract renewals and implementation handoffs. If those records support the switching-cost argument, proceed to valuation diligence. If retention depends on concessions or continuing custom work, revisit the thesis before moving forward.
